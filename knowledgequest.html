Introduction

Communication and Media Literacy is a subject that helps us understand how we communicate with other people and how we use different forms of media in our daily lives. It teaches us how to express our thoughts and ideas clearly, listen to others, and understand the messages we see and hear.

Nowadays, we get information from social media, television, the internet, and other platforms. Because of this, it is important to know how to identify whether the information we see is true or fake. This subject helps us become better communicators, responsible media users, and critical thinkers in our everyday lives.

Learning Objectives

At the end of the lesson, students are expected to:

  1. Learn what communication means and why it is important in our daily lives.
  2. Learn how to check information and recognize the difference between facts, opinions, and fake news.
  3. Understand the importance of being respectful, careful, and responsible when using social media and other online platforms.

Basic communication

1. Elements of the communication

  • Sender: The person who starts the message.
  • Message: The idea, thought, or information being shared.
  • Encoding: Turning thoughts into words, symbols, or actions.
  • Channel: The medium used to send the message, such as speech, text, or media.
  • Receiver: The person who gets the message.
  • Decoding: Interpreting or giving meaning to the message.
  • Feedback: The receiver's response back to the sender.

2.Types and Methods of Communication

  • Verbal communication: Using spoken or written words to share meaning.
  • Non-verbal communication: Expressing meaning through gestures, facial expressions, posture, tone, and body language.
  • Visual communication: Using images, charts, signs, symbols, or videos to convey ideas.
  • Communication contexts: Interactions ranging from intrapersonal (self) and interpersonal (one-on-one or small groups) to public and mass communication

3.Barriers and Effective Skills

  • Barriers (noise): Physical distractions, language differences, emotional states, or unclear wording that block understanding.
  • Listening and feedback: Paying active attention and giving clear, timely, and polite responses.
  • Foundational writing: Using clear grammar, punctuation, sentence structure, and concise wording.
